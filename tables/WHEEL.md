# The Wheel

A physical spinner whose segments are **table names**. Spin it and it tells you what kind of thing you are adding to the map. Then roll on that table, pick from it, or ignore it and use the idea you just had.

The wheel is one half of a permanent fork: 🎡 **Spin** or 🎯 **Choose**. See [why it exists](../rulebook/01-how-to-play.md#why-the-wheel-exists) — it is an accessibility mechanism, not a randomizer, and it is the reason several other rules in this game look softer than they need to be.

## Suggested segment sets by Age

Not every table belongs on the wheel in every Age. A wheel that can land on Artifacts in the Primordial Age is a wheel that produces a shrug.

| Age | Segments to include |
|---|---|
| I. Primordial | Mundane Terrain, Magical Terrain, Dramatic Features, Sources of Magic |
| II. Prehistoric | the above, plus Peoples, Avatars, Significant Species, Significant Beings, Disasters, Events |
| III. Ancient | drop Mundane Terrain, add Magical Structures, Significant Locales & Items, Artifacts & Relics |
| IV. Present | all of them |

## Building one

Any spinner works. Label the segments, spin, read it. A physical wheel with a click to it beats a digital roller, because the point is the small ceremony of starting, not the quality of the entropy.

**Open question:** whether the published edition ships a printed wheel, a card deck that does the same job, or just instructions for making one. Card draws are cheaper to produce and easier to weight per Age; a wheel is better at the table. Not decided — see [`../design/OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`](../design/OPEN-QUESTIONS.md).
