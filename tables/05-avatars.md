# Table 5: AVATARS

**100 entries.** Roll d100, or just pick. Making something up is always allowed.

| # | Name | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Wayfarer | A wandering mortal or near-mortal figure drawn to roads, thresholds, rumors, and unfinished business. Between great tasks they drift from place to place, collecting small obligations, strange maps, and people who decide they must be important. |
| 2 | The Pilgrim Knight | A sworn traveler in armor, robe, or ceremonial burden, created to carry law, faith, vengeance, or protection into distant places. When not in motion they keep vigil at shrines, ruins, or crossroads, awaiting the next command with unnerving patience. |
| 3 | The Masked Envoy | A humanoid bearer of purpose whose true face is unknown or perhaps absent. They move between courts, camps, and hidden powers, often serving as messenger, negotiator, or omen, and in idle periods they practice speeches for futures that have not happened yet. |
| 4 | The Exile Prince | A displaced heir, claimant, or ceremonial ruler who has outlived the realm that made them. They travel with scraps of courtly behavior and impossible dignity, treating every campfire like a temporary throne room until called to something worthy. |
| 5 | The Dust Prophet | A desert saint, ash-coated wanderer, or rag-wrapped visionary whose body has become proof of survival. They spend the quiet years muttering revelations to dunes, walls, and abandoned wells, then suddenly emerge to redirect history. |
| 6 | The Hollow Monk | A disciplined humanoid vessel who has emptied themselves of ordinary desire so purpose can inhabit them cleanly. In times of stillness they maintain severe routines, tend small gardens, copy symbols, or remain seated for weeks awaiting an inner summons. |
| 7 | The Battlefield Revenant | A dead warrior, standard-bearer, scout, or commander reconstituted by unfinished purpose. When not carrying out some terrible necessity they haunt old routes and forgotten forts, drilling ghosts, remembering formations, and refusing formal release. |
| 8 | The Shrine Child | A child, or childlike being, raised by omen, rite, or local devotion as the dwelling-place of something larger. In ordinary time they may seem playful, distracted, or innocent, though entire communities quietly structure themselves around their moods and declarations. |
| 9 | The Ashen Widow | A human-shaped avatar born from catastrophe, mourning, or the destruction of a great house. They wander with relics sewn into their clothing and often spend dormant years gathering fragments, names, and debts from the ashes of old worlds. |
| 10 | The Beggar-Saint | A ragged, itinerant, physically unimpressive vessel of improbable authority. They linger in markets, doorways, and temple steps, accepting alms they do not need, speaking rarely, and intervening only when a moment has become spiritually expensive. |
| 11 | The Lantern Hermit | A solitary keeper of a tower, cave, bridge, signal-fire, or impossible light. They seem to exist mainly to maintain readiness, as though the world periodically requires exactly one person to still be awake, watching, and willing to answer. |
| 12 | The Beast-Talker | A humanoid intermediary between the speaking and unspoken world, at ease among animals, monsters, and things that move in herds. Between missions they often vanish into wilderness, acting as mediator, judge, or gossip-broker among nonhuman lives. |
| 13 | The Dream Walker | A person whose sleep is as consequential as another’s waking life. They may spend long inactive seasons bedridden, wandering visions, sending messages, making bargains, or preparing movements that only become visible once they rise again. |
| 14 | The God-Touched Vessel | A mortal body that has, at some point, clearly and disastrously contained more divinity than it was built for. Even at rest they carry residue: scars of radiance, unusual gravity, impossible calm, or habits that suggest they are listening to someone else. |
| 15 | The Self-Crowned One | A person who declared themselves necessary and, against reason, reality agreed. They spend dormant periods accumulating followers, symbols, and protocols, rehearsing the next moment when their private certainty becomes public fact. |
| 16 | The Cinder Nun | A cloistered, ascetic, or vow-bound figure linked to fire, destruction, purification, or ending. When not active in the great pattern, they keep records of what must someday be burned, released, forgiven, or reduced to honest ash. |
| 17 | The Star Reader | A scholar-wanderer whose body and fate are aligned to celestial patterns. In quiet years they chart skies, alter calendars, and wait for conjunctions, sometimes treating entire decades as merely preparatory gestures. |
| 18 | The Courtless Judge | A humanoid avatar of verdict, discernment, or consequence who no longer belongs to any state. They spend their idle existence listening to disputes in inns, roadside camps, and ruined halls, passing binding judgments whether invited or not. |
| 19 | The Pilgrim Bride | A ceremonial figure joined not to a person but to a river, road, mountain, season, god, or fate. Their life between assignments is full of ritual preparation, symbolic hospitality, and the ongoing maintenance of a relationship the rest of the world barely understands. |
| 20 | The Salt Admiral | An old mariner, commander, or sea-borne dignitary who serves coasts, storms, currents, and submerged powers more than nations. In fallow periods they inspect harbors, mutter to tides, and wait for the sea to issue an order worth obeying. |
| 21 | The Great Stag | A towering horned beast of wilderness sovereignty, migration, sacrifice, or royal omen. Between great interventions it leads hidden herds, appears to the lost, tramples sacred boundaries, and vanishes before anyone can decide whether it was animal or god. |
| 22 | The White Hart | A miraculous deer-like being associated with pursuit, destiny, and the invitation to leave ordinary life behind. It spends long stretches simply being glimpsed at the wrong moment, drawing the ready away from safety and toward necessity. |
| 23 | The Black Hound | A death-hound, oath-hound, or road-hound that tracks promises, fugitives, or souls rather than scent alone. When not actively hunting, it lingers at graveyards, toll roads, and abandoned gates, as if waiting for the next command to ripen. |
| 24 | The Sun Lion | A lion, lion-spirit, or leonine colossus embodying rulership, guardianship, or unbearable splendor. In its dormant mode it lies in ruins, on warm stone, or beneath ceremonial trees, appearing asleep while radiating the threat of sudden intervention. |
| 25 | The Moon Wolf | A wolf of omen, kinship, and nocturnal purpose, often tied to thresholds between village and wild. During quiet eras it shadows travelers, adopts packs, patrols forgotten boundaries, and seems to be assessing whether a time of change has arrived. |
| 26 | The Grave Bear | An immense bear associated with memory, burial places, endurance, and winter authority. It spends inactive years in caves, tomb valleys, and old battlefields, sleeping beside histories too heavy for human custodians. |
| 27 | The River Serpent | A long-bodied creature of currents, passage, fertility, or submerged law. When not carrying out some large directive it coils in deep channels, rearranges crossings, watches ferrymen, and takes personal interest in those who move people from one shore to another. |
| 28 | The Storm Roc | A vast bird of wind, altitude, lightning, or omen-bearing flight. In ordinary time it circles inaccessible peaks and cloudbanks, sometimes lifting single chosen people or objects as though the sky itself had accepted courier duties. |
| 29 | The Carrion Crow-King | A crow, raven, or corvid sovereign of battlefield knowledge, rumor, salvage, and aftermath. It rules by gathering the useful from endings, and in peacetime it busies itself with messages, secrets, shiny fragments, and the emotional debris of empires. |
| 30 | The Cave Tortoise | An ancient shelled being whose slowness is strategic rather than passive. It spends centuries sheltering pilgrims, maps, seeds, or relics within or beneath itself, moving only when the age has become ready for one deliberate change. |
| 31 | The Moss Mammoth | A colossal, furred, half-landscape creature carrying age, memory, and migratory significance. When not serving some singular purpose it follows routes older than kingdoms, creating pilgrim cultures, grasslands, and myths in its wake. |
| 32 | The Ember Fox | A fox, jackal, or small clever predator associated with trickery, survival, hearthfire theft, or bright opportunism. In its idle seasons it steals the right object from the wrong person and seems to be rearranging coincidence for later use. |
| 33 | The Shore Leviathan | A whale-like, scaled, or impossible marine presence whose appearances mark eras, disasters, and divine attention. Much of the time it simply moves in distant waters, drawing cults, songs, fisheries, and prophecies into orbit around itself. |
| 34 | The Mire Heron | A tall wetland wader embodying patience, hidden violence, and the stillness before action. It may stand unmoving for days in swamp or estuary, becoming part of the landscape until the moment it spears exactly the thing fate required. |
| 35 | The Antlered Boar | A furious hybrid force of forest invasion, stubbornness, and impossible charge. In periods of quiet it uproots boundaries, ruins overgrown roads, and becomes the local explanation for why some places cannot remain civilized. |
| 36 | The Thousand-Eyed Owl | A nocturnal watcher associated with wisdom, surveillance, omens, and the disturbing possibility of being truly known. It spends its downtime in towers, hollow trees, and abandoned temples, assembling a map of secrets no one asked it to keep. |
| 37 | The Glass Moth | A luminous winged creature of obsession, metamorphosis, attraction, and fatal beauty. When not deployed by larger purpose it haunts lanterns, libraries, mirrors, and sleeping faces, nudging fragile transformations forward. |
| 38 | The Iron Hart | A metallic, antlered beast associated with kingship, war-readiness, and the sacred weight of bearing burdens. In still years it roams old roads and mountain passes, allowing no one to tame it but occasionally choosing who may follow. |
| 39 | The Pale Hare | A moon-marked rabbit or hare of luck, warning, speed, and brief impossible appearances. It acts like a small thing but reliably arrives at moments when history must either split or be escaped. |
| 40 | The Red Mare | A horse-spirit, steppe sovereign, or war-messenger associated with velocity, omen-riding, and the carrying of chosen people. Between commands it appears in dreams, storms, and lonely fields, always just near enough to be mistaken for ordinary. |
| 41 | The Hearth Kobold | A small cunning domestic spirit attached to ovens, workshops, storehouses, or hidden household continuity. Much of its existence is spent maintaining tiny systems, stealing the right nail, and protecting the line between inhabited shelter and mere building. |
| 42 | The Vault Goblin | A scavenger-intelligence of hoards, ledgers, forgotten tunnels, and badly guarded value. It may seem petty until the world needs someone perfectly adapted to locate, steal, or preserve one irreplaceable item. |
| 43 | The Bridge Troll | A threshold creature of tolls, passage, negotiation, and stubborn local sovereignty. In quiet times it maintains customs of exchange at rivers, gates, culverts, and ravines, enforcing ancient etiquette no empire successfully abolished. |
| 44 | The Lamp Gnome | A diminutive maker-spirit associated with tools, maintenance, small miracles, and the belief that every great undertaking survives by hidden craft. Between large assignments it fixes impossible machinery and quarrels with the careless. |
| 45 | The Garden Brownie | A domestic or cottage-bound spirit that extends its care to orchards, terraces, walls, and cultivated wildness. When left undisturbed it improves everything a little; when offended it becomes a patient saboteur of comfort. |
| 46 | The Hill Dwarf Ancestor | A compact, embodied ancestor-being of stone halls, craft, grudges, and inherited duty. In dormant cycles it reappears in clan places, mine roads, and family workshops to correct errors and remember what descendants conveniently forgot. |
| 47 | The Wren-Faced Sprite | A tiny winged or birdlike personification of messages, mischief, and overlooked openings. It spends its time nesting in archives, rafters, and shrines, moving small but crucial things from one hand to another. |
| 48 | The Hearth Hag | An old, liminal woman-spirit of practical magic, domestic law, cursing, and protection bought at a cost. When not shaping major outcomes she manages local bargains, births, thresholds, and vengeance with village-scale precision. |
| 49 | The Moss Goblin Choir | A clutch or colony of small beings that count as one avatar when they act in perfect concert. In quiet years they farm rumors, fungi, bones, or shiny objects, waiting until a larger will arranges them into sudden coherence. |
| 50 | The Threshold Impling | A mischievous lesser entity living in cracks, doors, attics, shrines, and the little gap before events begin. It spends most of its existence preparing minor accidents and timely opportunities for the day it is needed in earnest. |
| 51 | The River Nymph | A local feminine, fluid, or simply river-shaped spirit of place, allure, guidance, and consequence. She may spend long eras attending one bend, ford, spring, or pool, quietly selecting who may pass transformed. |
| 52 | The Dryad | A tree-bound or grove-bound intelligence that can move when purpose requires but prefers rooted continuity. In dormant times it concerns itself with growth, memory, trespass, and the long emotional life of forests. |
| 53 | The Oread | A mountain, cliff, or highland spirit with a body like stone made briefly mobile. It appears when slopes, passes, avalanches, or old heights need an advocate, and otherwise retreats into patient geological listening. |
| 54 | The Nereid | A sea-edge or wave-born avatar of coastlines, inlets, coves, and dangerous beauty. She spends inactive centuries teaching the same rocks how to receive the same tides until something significant finally comes ashore. |
| 55 | The Undine | A more elemental water-being than a mere local spirit: rain-fed, spring-fed, fountain-fed, and capable of taking many forms. Between commands it slips through systems of wells, pipes, caves, and bloodlines alike. |
| 56 | The Sylph | A creature of upper air, whisper, velocity, and impossible movement, often only partly embodied. It spends its idle existence carrying voices, changing weather-moods, and practicing entry into rooms that were thought closed. |
| 57 | The Salamander | A fire-being in the old sense: not a lizard merely, but an intelligence of flame, furnace, ember, and transmutation. When not sent to alter events, it inhabits kilns, hearths, pyres, forges, and the hidden heat inside waiting things. |
| 58 | The Gnome of Deep Earth | An elemental intelligence of strata, ore, pressure, and buried pattern, sometimes squat and personlike, sometimes not. It spends most of its time arranging subterranean realities until some surface crisis requires ancient material intervention. |
| 59 | The Mist Bride | A figure assembled from fog, river-breath, or coastal haze, associated with concealment, seduction, and uncertain borders. In quiet seasons she wanders dawnlands and marshes, gathering stories people accidentally tell the half-seen. |
| 60 | The Thorn Maiden | A floral or briar-wrapped spirit of defensive growth, seasonal violence, and protected secrets. She is most active when something ought not be approached, and least active when all boundaries are being respected. |
| 61 | The Dune Whisperer | A spirit of sand movement, burial, shifting routes, and the memory of places erased by wind. It passes quiet centuries rearranging evidence, preserving ruins, and waiting for the one traveler who must find what no map can keep. |
| 62 | The Fen Mother | A marsh or bog intelligence associated with preservation, slow hunger, hidden paths, and deep old remembering. When not acting on a grander will, she receives offerings, keeps dangerous things from surfacing, and occasionally changes her mind. |
| 63 | The Glacier Maid | A cold, severe being of accumulation, grinding change, and time measured in pressure rather than breath. Her interventions are rare and enormous; otherwise she resides in crevasse, meltwater, and silence. |
| 64 | The Volcano Child | A bright, unstable, half-playful manifestation of subterranean force and inevitable eruption. It is as likely to build delicate sculptures of ash as to destroy a city, and often treats both as versions of expression. |
| 65 | The Salt Wife | A coastal or desert-crust spirit of preservation, thirst, memory, and harsh clarity. In inactive years she cures food, mummifies truths, and watches who can endure being stripped down to what remains. |
| 66 | The Rain Kinglet | A small but sovereign weather-spirit tied to sudden showers, fertility, relief, and mood-breaking mercy. It seems whimsical until entire harvests, migrations, or wars turn on where it chooses to weep. |
| 67 | The Echo Spirit | An inhabiting intelligence of repetition, resonance, unfinished speech, and places that keep saying what happened there. Most of its life is spent in canyons, halls, wells, and empty rooms, curating which sounds are allowed to survive. |
| 68 | The Candle Ghost | A small dead thing with just enough identity left to carry a specific light forward. It lingers around vigils, sickbeds, archives, and thresholds, often serving purposes far larger than its fragile appearance suggests. |
| 69 | The Procession Dead | Not one ghost but a recurring parade of the same dead office: mourners, guards, musicians, or attendants of an old duty. They appear when the world needs ceremony restored and otherwise continue rehearsing in invisible routes. |
| 70 | The Mourning Lady | A sorrow-shaped specter whose grief has become socially and metaphysically useful. She may haunt one loss for centuries, but when called upon she can redirect mourning itself into warning, blessing, curse, or communal memory. |
| 71 | The Battlefield Shade | A leaner, more tactical ghost than a revenant: scout, skirmisher, banner-shadow, or last witness. It passes its idle years surveying old killing grounds and learning every route by which violence returns. |
| 72 | The Corpse Oracle | A dead or death-adjacent body through which visions, judgments, or instructions are delivered. Between active revelations it lies still in crypt, cart, swamp, shrine, or cave, attended by those willing to wait out the smell and the truth. |
| 73 | The Empty Armor | A spirit inhabiting or intermittently using a suit of arms, regalia, or ceremonial shell. At rest it stands in hall, tomb, or ruin as decoration until a purpose awakens inside the metal. |
| 74 | The Candle-Crowned Ancestor | An ancestral dead figure who returns not in terror but in office, carrying household, clan, or dynastic obligations. In ordinary time they advise dreams, supervise rituals, and judge descendants with exhausting accuracy. |
| 75 | The Grave Choir | A collective undead intelligence made of many retained voices rather than many retained bodies. It is most often encountered as song, liturgy, warning, or layered speech, and only rarely condenses into visible form. |
| 76 | The Pale Ferryman | A death-border avatar associated with crossings, tolls, last journeys, and the etiquette of departure. Between major interventions they continue mundane work, because the dead do not stop requiring logistics just because history is quiet. |
| 77 | The Barrow King | A tomb sovereign ruling over treasure, oath-bones, and sealed histories beneath the earth. In dormant eras they remain magnificently buried, governing the dead with more competence than many kings govern the living. |
| 78 | The Unquiet Child | A smaller ghostly being of unfinished need, uncanny innocence, and devastating selectivity. It spends long stretches playing in the edges of households and ruins until the exact person arrives who must not be allowed to leave unchanged. |
| 79 | The Star-Fallen | A being from the sky, or from whatever lies beyond the sky, not fully adapted to local reality. In still periods it studies gravity, language, and mortality with anthropological fascination, while hidden cults form around its errors. |
| 80 | The Moon-Torn | An avatar altered by lunar power, cyclical identity, reflected light, and the instability of becoming. Between great acts it lives in phases, sometimes coherent, sometimes multiple, always awaiting the night in which it matters again. |
| 81 | The Comet Herald | A bright transient being whose appearances coincide with dynastic change, impossible births, strange inventions, or collapse. It is built for rare arrival, so most of its existence is spent as rumor, omen, or preparation. |
| 82 | The Void Pilgrim | A cloaked traveler aligned to absence, distance, silence between stars, and the spaces ordinary meaning does not reach. In inactive times they visit edges: cliffs, observatories, exhausted temples, forgotten stations of thought. |
| 83 | The Aurora Saint | A radiant being of colored light, high latitudes, communication, wonder, and weaponized beauty. When not called to world-scale work it drifts above remote places, teaching visions to those who can survive awe without mistaking it for ownership. |
| 84 | The Meteor Beast | A living impact, crater-born animal, or foreign predator carrying shock, suddenness, and alien mineral will. It often sleeps buried until the age provides a target worthy of waking it. |
| 85 | The Eclipse Courser | A horse-, hound-, or rider-shaped celestial avatar of interruption, omen-shadow, and temporary inversion. It arrives when one order must pass in darkness so another can emerge, then vanishes before explanations regain authority. |
| 86 | The Constellation Shepherd | A star-linked guide who tends routes, destinies, lost travelers, and named patterns in the sky. Their ordinary years are spent teaching navigation to fools and rearranging symbolic correspondences for later use. |
| 87 | The Flesh Golem | An assembled body animated by purpose more than soul, whether made by craft, ritual, grief, or necessity. Between missions it often seeks tasks, repairs, and identities, trying to decide whether service is enough to count as a life. |
| 88 | The Clay Servitor | A shaped earthen body meant for labor, guardianship, memory-keeping, or obedience, though age has made it eccentric. In quiet times it tends ancient instructions with great seriousness, even when the world that wrote them is gone. |
| 89 | The Clockwork Herald | A brass, wooden, or impossible mechanical being built to deliver law, warning, timing, or exact intervention. Dormant periods are spent winding itself, preserving schedules, and resenting improvisation. |
| 90 | The Living Idol | A statue, sacred image, or cult object that can move, speak, or act when enough significance accumulates. Most of the time it holds ritual stillness, receiving prayers, dust, and politics until movement becomes unavoidable. |
| 91 | The Bronze Colossus | A giant artificial guardian or war-form associated with protection, memory of empire, and overwhelming physical authority. When inactive it resembles architecture, which is often how civilizations keep making the mistake of building around it. |
| 92 | The Wax Bridegroom | A beautiful but unstable constructed being of intimacy, ceremony, and temporary perfection. It may melt, deform, or renew according to context, making it especially suited to missions involving deception, sacrifice, or impossible fidelity. |
| 93 | The Rune Engine | A semi-personal machine whose identity is distributed across inscriptions, moving parts, and the logic of its making. It spends dormant eras processing inputs no one remembers giving, until suddenly producing the one output history needed. |
| 94 | The Mirror Double | An avatar born from reflection, imitation, and selective divergence from an original being. When not employed by larger purpose it studies, rehearses, and improves upon the self it was once meant only to echo. |
| 95 | The Skin-Changer | A mutable vessel capable of taking on several bodies, species, genders, ages, or symbolic forms as function requires. Much of its inactive life is spent experimenting with personhood and deciding which masks have become real. |
| 96 | The Chimera | A composite creature of multiple animal, spiritual, or conceptual lineages, carrying contradiction as a stable condition. Between assignments it often withdraws to remote places simply to remain itself without being interpreted by lesser minds. |
| 97 | The Library Beast | A creature whose body stores words, symbols, contracts, maps, or histories in fur, scales, feathers, bone, or internal chambers. It is both archive and actor, spending quiet eras curating what must be remembered when the world grows careless. |
| 98 | The House That Walks | A mobile dwelling-being, hut-spirit, ambulatory sanctuary, or architectural avatar carrying people, relics, or stories across ages. In peaceful times it settles briefly in useful places, becoming inn, shrine, orphanage, embassy, or legend as needed. |
| 99 | The Probability Child | A being around whom coincidence thickens into pattern and impossibility becomes administratively normal. They spend idle periods seeming absurdly lucky, but the older powers know they are really being positioned. |
| 100 | Roll Twice | This avatar carries two natures at once, either obviously or in secret. Decide whether the combination is harmonious, unstable, inherited, engineered, or the central problem of the setting. |
