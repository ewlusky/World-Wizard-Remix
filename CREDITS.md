# Credits and attribution

## This game is a hack

This project began as a house variant of **[Worldwizard](https://lampblack-brimstone.itch.io/worldwizard)** by **Lampblack & Brimstone**, a print-and-play collaborative worldbuilding game for 1-8 players.

**If you like what's here, buy the original first.** It is $5 or more on itch.io and it is where this started.

### What came from Worldwizard

The foundation this is built on:

- The **Steward** role — players as caretakers of a world rather than characters in it
- The **hex map** as the shared, physical game object
- The **Action Point** economy for taking turns
- The **Age progression** structure, moving the world forward through named historical eras
- **Reference tables** as creative prompts
- The core premise: no GM, no preset world, collaborative construction across sessions

### What was developed here

This variant grew out of playing the original with a household that included players who had never done worldbuilding and were not sure they wanted to. Most of what follows exists to solve that specific problem — keeping a long collaborative game welcoming to someone who did not come in excited about it.

- **Logarithmic Age scaling** — seven Ages instead of four, each with a shrinking "Now," so a turn spans eons early and a single season by the end
- **The Quiet Turn** — a free, ungated contribution requiring no Action Points, so a passing player can add a detail without committing to a full turn
- **The Weave Bonus** — building on another Steward's creation costs nothing extra and rewards *them*, which turns the game cooperative at the mechanical level
- **Complicate** — spend nothing, gain an Action Point, and make one of your own creations messier: a flaw, a contradiction, a private sorrow
- **The Price of Passing** — passing is allowed, but the resulting disaster lands on something you made
- **The Kith layer** — named ordinary mortals as first-class worldbuilding material, weighted equally with gods and dynasties
- **The Language mechanic** — Peoples develop vocabulary through play; coined words outlive the Peoples who coined them, and languages can drift and die
- **Genus Loci** — once per Age, speak *as* a place, after which it becomes a named entity that accrues history
- **Fade rather than elimination** — creations that fail their survival roll get one final legendary act instead of simply being erased
- **The One-Minute Rules** — the entire game on a single page, with everything else as reference

Design rationale for each of these lives in [`design/`](design/).

### Other games this learned from

Studied during design, with notes in [`reference/GAMES-RESEARCH.md`](reference/GAMES-RESEARCH.md):

- **Microscope** (Ben Robbins) — the Palette mechanic, which solves content boundaries for mixed-age tables
- **The Quiet Year** (Avery Alder) — the map as accumulating artifact
- **Dawn of Worlds** — the Action Point and Age framework this genre shares
- **Wanderhome** (Jay Dragon) — token economy, and the idea that small lives carry weight
