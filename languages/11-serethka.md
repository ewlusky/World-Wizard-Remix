## Cultural Context

Serethka is the tongue of the Marsh Covenant — hedge witches, spirit-speakers, and herbalists who live in the great swamps and wetlands. It is a language of aspect rather than tense: Serethka speakers do not care when something happened, but rather whether it is complete, ongoing, or repeatedly recurring. The language also has a dedicated "shadow voice" — a grammatical construction for speaking about things that exist partially: rumors, spirits, memories, and spells being cast.

## Phonology & Grammar Rules

1. Slavic consonant clusters: str, zhr, br, kl, gr, vr, dr, shch (written šč), zhn. These are not simplified — all consonants are pronounced.

2. Vowels: a, e, i, o, u, and the Slavic ë (soft "yo" sound) and ă (reduced schwa). Vowels alternate with consonant clusters in a distinctive rhythm.

3. Grammatical aspect (not tense!) is mandatory: every verb must be either perfective (completed, marked with the prefix za- or po-) or imperfective (ongoing, no prefix). "I wrote" vs "I was writing" is the core distinction.

4. The "shadow voice" for partially real things: the particle ksha- before the noun or verb marks it as: spirit-realm, rumor, memory, dream, or active spell. "Ksha-voda" = the water that-is-also-a-spirit / the spirit in the form of water.

5. Cases: Nominative (subject), Accusative (object, -u/-yu suffix), Genitive (possession, -y/-i), Dative (to/for, -e/-i), Instrumental (with/by means of, -oy/-ey), Locative (in/on/at, -e/-i). Feminine nouns end in -a; masculine in consonants; neuter in -o.

6. The collective plural (-stv- infix) expresses a group acting as one: vedma = witch; vedmastv = the witches acting collectively as one force.

7. Diminutives are formed with -ka/-ka: vedma → vedmaka (little witch / young witch). Augmentatives use -ina: vedma → vedmina (great witch / the crone).

8. Negation doubles: ne + the verb + nichego (nothing) or nigde (nowhere). "Ne znayu nichego" = "I do not know nothing" = strong "I know nothing."

## Phrases & Expressions

| Phrase | Literal Translation | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Ksha-dukh gorit nochka-de | "A half-spirit burns in the little night" | A spell is forming / something is awakening |
| Vedmastv zagorela | "The coven burned completely (it is done)" | The ritual is complete; we are one |
| Ne znayu nichego zmey-a | "I know nothing of the serpent" | I refuse to speak of this; or: I genuinely know nothing |
| Zhmr-ë vedma gorit | "A witch burns in the swamp" | Ongoing — a witch is working her craft there now |
| Ksha-voda pomnet | "The spirit-water remembers" | The swamp knows all that has happened here |
| Travnik brzhot-oy pogovorit | "The herbalist speaks by means of poison" | The medicine-worker uses dangerous knowledge wisely |
| Strega koshmar-u dast | "The old witch gives to the nightmare" | She feeds the bad dreams to keep the village safe (protective ritual) |

## Idioms & Cultural Sayings

| Idiom | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Zhmr pamyataet — "The swamp remembers" | The land holds the memory of all that happened on it. Do not assume your deeds are forgotten. |
| Vedma ne gorit, vedma zharit — "A witch does not burn, a witch roasts" | Said mockingly of accusations against witches. They cannot be simply destroyed. |
| Ksha brak nichego — "There is no 'shadow nothing'" | Every spirit exists for a reason. Nothing haunts without cause. |
| Zmey stariy, zmey mudry — "An old serpent is a wise serpent" | Respect age and experience. Those who have survived long have learned something. |
| Brzhot-oy gorit serdtse — "The heart burns with poison" | Said of someone consumed by resentment or obsession. Dangerous, not foolish. |
