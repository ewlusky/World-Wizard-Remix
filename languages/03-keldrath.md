## Cultural Context

Keldrath is spoken by the Keldrathi — a mountain-dwelling people of master smiths and tunnel-engineers. The language reflects their values: precision over poetry, endurance over grace. Keldrath is agglutinative, meaning words grow by stacking suffixes and prefixes rather than changing internal vowels. A single Keldrath word can express what English requires an entire sentence to say.

## Phonology & Grammar Rules

1. Keldrath permits complex consonant clusters that would be impossible in most languages: brk, gzd, thv, skr, ndm. These are pronounced with short connecting "half-vowels."

2. Primary vowels are: a (as in "ash"), e (as in "bed"), i (as in "bit"), o (as in "old"), u (as in "full"). No diphthongs.

3. Stress always falls on the first syllable of the root word, regardless of affixes added.

4. Nouns decline for five cases: subject (-ak suffix), object (-om), tool/instrument (-ren), origin/source (-thal), and destination/purpose (-drath).

5. Words are built by compounding roots. Grammar is primarily positional: the noun performing the action comes first, then the verb-root, then the noun being acted upon. Modifiers always precede what they modify.

6. Diminutives are formed with the prefix zi-: keld (mountain) → zikeld (hill, little mountain). Augmentatives use the prefix groth-: keld → grothkeld (mountain range, great mountains).

7. Aspect is expressed through verb suffixes: -om (completed), -ak (in progress), -ren (habitual), -drath (intended future).

8. There is no separate word for "yes" or "no." Agreement is expressed by repeating the verb; disagreement by repeating the verb with the negation prefix brak-: "Will you forge?" "I forge." / "I do not-forge."

## Idioms & Cultural Sayings

| Idiom | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Skar-om, vel-ak — "The fire is done, the steel endures" | The difficult time has passed; what remains is what was forged in it. Said to console the grieving. |
| Thr drüm vel — "Stone drinks steel" | The mountain consumes those who work it. A warning about obsession with craft consuming the craftsman. |
| Brüthom brak drom — "A veteran has no depth" | Someone who's been everywhere and has seen everything loses the capacity for wonder. Not always an insult. |
| Grothkeld zi-vel — "The great mountain makes small steel" | Grand ambitions often produce modest results. A critique of overreachers. |
| Klendrak lō — "The cooling has come" | A relationship, idea, or phase of life has reached its end and is now fixed. Can be sad or peaceful. |
