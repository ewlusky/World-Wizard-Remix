## Cultural Context

The Zhevakand-speaking peoples are the great horseback nomads of the world — traders, scouts, and occasional raiders who have no permanent settlements. Their language reflects two obsessions: precision (what is the source of your knowledge?) and hospitality (who is in your care?). The language has a grammaticalized evidentiality system — every verb must be marked for how the speaker knows what they know. Lying is linguistically visible in Zhevakand because lying requires you to mark as "witnessed" what you did not witness.

## Phonology & Grammar Rules

1. Vowel harmony: Back vowels (a, o, u, ı) and front vowels (e, ö, ü, i) do not mix in a native word. Suffixes change their vowels to match the root.

2. Consonant harmony: voiced consonants (b, d, g, z) follow voiced; unvoiced (p, t, k, s) follow unvoiced. This applies within suffixes.

3. Evidentiality suffixes on every verb (mandatory): -dim (I witnessed it directly); -miş (I was told / I infer); -ken (it was happening when I arrived); -acak (I intend / expect this will happen); -malı (I believe this should be true).

4. Agglutination: suffixes stack in order (verb root + tense + evidentiality + person + question marker): zhev-di-m = "I rode (and saw it happen)"; zhev-miş-im = "I am told I rode / they say I rode (I don't remember)."

5. The suffix -kan marks sacred or ancestral knowledge — things known because the ancestors knew them. It carries the most authority.

6. Possessives are suffixes: -ım/im (my), -ın/in (your), -ı/i (their). Horse = at; my horse = atım; your horse = atın.

7. Questions add -mı/-mi/-mu/-mü at the end depending on vowel harmony: atın mı? (Is it your horse?).

8. Honorific: The particle bey (masculine) or khân (any gender, highest honor) is placed after a name: Zhevak-bey, Kandara-khân.

## Phrases & Expressions

| Phrase | Literal Translation | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| At zhev-di-m tengri kök-de | "I personally watched the horse ride under the divine sky" | I witnessed this great thing with my own eyes |
| Dost yurt-ım-da | "Friend, in my home" | You are welcome here; this is your home now |
| Boran miş-kan tengri | "The storm is said to come from the sky-ancestors" | A danger forewarned by tradition / heed old wisdom |
| Kurultay acak-mı? | "Will a council be called? (I expect)" | Asking if leadership will convene |
| Şaman ötük-dim | "I personally heard the shaman's petition" | I was present at the ceremony; this is reliable |
| Düşman kand-ında malı? | "Should an enemy be in our territory?" | This should not be happening — someone answer for this |
| Altan dost dim-miş | "I saw with my eyes a golden friend (but others told me too)" | Said of a rare and trusted companion — doubly verified |

## Idioms & Cultural Sayings

| Idiom | Meaning |
|---|---|
| At bilen yol bulur — "The horse that knows, finds the road" | Trust in those with experience. An expert knows their domain without being told. |
| Boran olmadan Tengri bilinmez — "Without a storm, the sky is not known" | You only discover the divine and your own character under pressure. Hardship is revelation. |
| Yurt taşınır, kand taşınmaz — "The home moves, the land does not" | Home is where your people are, not a fixed place. Used to comfort those who must migrate. |
| Şaman iki yerde — "The shaman is in two places" | Said of someone who speaks for two parties at once, especially in negotiation. Can be admiring or suspicious. |
| Dost atı ödünç verir — "A friend loans their horse" | True friendship is measured in sacrifice. Would they give you their most precious thing? |
