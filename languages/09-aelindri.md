## Cultural Context

Aelindri is an ancient sacred language used by star-priests, astrologers, and the keepers of celestial lore. It is not a language of the people but of the initiated — spoken in temples and observatories, used in prophecy and star-charts. It is exceptionally precise: every noun declines for seven cases, every verb conjugates for person, number, tense, mood, and whether the subject is mortal, immortal, or divine. To speak Aelindri incorrectly in a ritual context is considered not merely rude but potentially catastrophic.

## Phonology & Grammar Rules

1. Sounds are classical and clear: a, e, i, o, u (pure vowels); consonants l, n, m, r (trilled), s, t, k, v, and the aspirates th and kh.

2. Long vowels (marked with macrons: ā, ē, ī, ō, ū) are grammatically distinct from short vowels and change case meaning.

3. Stress falls on the penultimate syllable if it is heavy (contains a long vowel or two consonants); otherwise on the antepenultimate.

4. Noun cases: Nominative (subject, unmarked), Accusative (object, -em/-am), Genitive (possession, -is/-as), Dative (recipient, -ī/-ae), Ablative (separation/instrument, -ō/-ā), Locative (place, -ī/-ae), Vocative (address, varies).

5. Three noun genders: Celestial (masculine, for divine/male concepts), Terrestrial (feminine, for mortal/earthly), and Eternal (neuter, for timeless truths).

6. Verbs conjugate for: person (1/2/3), number (singular/plural/collective), tense (past/present/future), and aspect (mortal vs immortal vs divine action).

7. The divine aspect is marked by the prefix ae- and changes the meaning: aelind (a mortal who studies stars); aelindri (the sacred study itself). Ae- is only used by priests in formal ritual speech.

8. Double negation is standard. Questions are formed by -ne appended to the first word of the sentence.

## Phrases & Expressions

| Phrase | Literal Translation | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Stellā lūmine, sacerdōs vātēs | "By starlight, the priest prophesies" | Opening of a divination ritual |
| Ae-orbis fātum noctis | "The eternal sphere has written fate in the night" | A prophecy has been confirmed by celestial observation |
| Augūrium caeluminis vātēs-ne? | "Does the prophet read the omen of the sky-concept?" | Is the priest ready to interpret the sign? |
| Lūmen aetērnum, īnfra fātum | "Eternal light; mortal realm below fate" | What is above is timeless; what is below is determined |
| Fātum noctis stellā dīxit | "The fate of the night was spoken by the star" | This was prophesied; accept it |
| Sacerdōs ae-lūmen noctis-que diēsque | "The priest stands in the divine light of both night and day" | Said of a priest of great power who walks both worlds |
| Orbis aetērnum vātēs laudat | "The prophet praises the eternal sphere" | Ritual closing / thanksgiving |

## Idioms & Cultural Sayings

| Idiom | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Stellā cadunt, sacerdōs manet — "Stars fall, the priest remains" | Even as great powers pass away, the keeper of knowledge endures. Said of scholarly persistence. |
| Fātum īnfra, augūrium supra — "Fate below, omen above" | What is happening in the mortal world is reflected in signs above. Read the sky to understand your troubles. |
| Noctis diēsque — "Of night and of day" | Something that belongs to both worlds / a thing of paradox. Also used to mean "always, in every condition." |
| Lūmen noctis — "Light of night" | Used metaphorically for wisdom in dark times, or literally for someone who brings clarity to confusion. |
| Vātēs brak vātēs — "A prophet does not prophecy to a prophet" | Don't explain something to someone who already knows it. (Aelindri version of "preaching to the choir.") |
