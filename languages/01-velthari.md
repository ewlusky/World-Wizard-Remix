## Cultural Context

Spoken by the Velthari Empire — a vast sun-baked civiliziation spanning three deserts and a dozen oases. Velthari prizes poetic complexity; a skilled speaker layers meaning the way a jeweler layers gold. The language has three registers: Low Velthari (commerce and street), Mid Velthari (court and scholarship), and High Velthari (sacred texts and oaths).

## Phonology & Grammar Rules

1. Consonants favor pharyngeals and uvulars: kh (خ), gh (غ), q, r (rolled), and the glottal stop (ʾ written as an apostrophe).

2. Vowels are short (a, i, u) or long (ā, ī, ū, marked with a macron). Long vowels are never stressed on the same syllable twice in a row.

3. Word stress falls on the final syllable if it contains a long vowel or consonant cluster; otherwise on the penultimate syllable.

4. Feminine nouns end in -at or -ah. Masculine nouns are unmarked or end in -ān (collective plural).

5. Verb roots are tri-consonantal: three core consonants around which vowel patterns weave meaning. (e.g., root K-T-B: katab = he wrote; kitāb = book; kātib = scribe.)

6. Adjectives follow the noun they modify and agree in gender: zahr al-nar (fire-flower) vs. zahrāt al-nar (fire-flowers).

7. Negation is formed by the prefix lā- before verbs and the word mā before nouns.

8. Questions are marked by the particle hal- at the sentence opening, or by a rising vowel elongation at sentence end.

## Phrases & Expressions

| Phrase | Literal Translation | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Al-ʾamr bi'llāh | "The command belongs to fate" | Said when accepting bad news |
| Māʾ wa nār | "Water and fire" | Two opposed forces / impossible balance |
| Rūhī fī yadayk | "My soul is in your hands" | Pledge of absolute loyalty |
| Hal taʿrif al-ghayb? | "Do you know the Unseen?" | Are you wise beyond your years? |

| Bi-salām al-rīh | "Go with the peace of the wind" | Formal farewell |

## Idioms & Cultural Sayings

| Idiom | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Yadayn min al-nār — "Hands of fire" | Said of a smith, cook, or mage of great skill — they touch what would burn others. |
| Yakhrij min al-ghayb — "He walks out of the Unseen" | Someone who appears out of nowhere, often with uncanny knowledge or timing. |
| Qalb al-sarab — "Heart of the mirage" | A person who seems warm and generous but offers nothing real; a charming deceiver. |
| ʾAkala al-rīh — "He ate the wind" | Someone who chased an impossible goal and wasted their life on it. |
| Māʾ fī al-raml — "Water in the sand" | An investment or relationship that gives nothing back; pouring yourself into nothing. |
