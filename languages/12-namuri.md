## Cultural Context

Namuri is a contact language — a creole that formed in the great island-chain markets where Ul'nakhari navigators, Tessavori merchants, Orishaan traders, and local island peoples all needed to communicate. It is grammatically simple by design but expressively rich through context and tone. It has borrowed freely from all its parent languages and continues to evolve rapidly. Every island cluster has its own dialect, but the "Standard Namuri" of the great port cities is understood everywhere in the chain.

## Phonology & Grammar Rules

1. Namuri avoids difficult sounds deliberately: no tones, no consonant clusters, no case inflection. Every syllable is CV or CVC, and final consonants are limited to -m, -n, -ng, -k, -p.

2. Vowels: a, e, i, o, u — pronounced purely and consistently. No long vowels. No vowel harmony required.

3. Every word can function as noun, verb, or adjective based on position. "Besar" = big (adj.); "besar!" = Grow! Enlarge! (command); "si besar" = the big one (noun with si).

4. The particle si marks animate nouns (people, animals); the particle di marks place; yang marks a relative clause. "Si besar yang di laut" = "The big one who is at sea."

5. Tense is expressed by time words, not inflection: sudah (already / completed); mau (want to / future intention); sedang (currently in the middle of); slalu (always / habitual).

6. Plural is expressed by doubling the noun: orang (person) → orang-orang (people, many persons).

7. Questions are formed by adding kah to the front of the sentence or adding? intonation. "Sudah makan-kah?" = "Have you eaten already?"

8. Negation: tidak (no/not for verbs); bukan (no/not for nouns). "Tidak makan" = not eating; "bukan orang" = not a person.

## Phrases & Expressions

| Phrase | Literal Translation | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Baik, teman? | "Good, friend?" | How are you? (casual, universal greeting) |
| Sudah makan-kah? | "Have you eaten already?" | Standard greeting — wellbeing check |
| Mau jual apa? | "Want to trade what?" | What are you selling? (market opener) |
| Boleh datang sini | "May come here" | You're welcome here / please come in |
| Tidak besar, tidak kecil — pas! | "Not big, not small — just right!" | This is the right amount / perfect deal |
| Teman di laut, teman di darat | "Friend at sea, friend on land" | A true and lasting alliance |
| Mau pulang, sudah jual | "I want to go home, already traded" | My business is done; I'm leaving satisfied |

## Idioms & Cultural Sayings

| Idiom | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Laut tidak ingat — "The sea does not remember" | What happens between islands stays between islands. Privacy between trading partners; a code of discretion. |
| Orang-orang jual, orang-orang makan — "People trade, people eat" | The simple truth of the market: everyone is here for the same reason. No shame in commerce. |
| Teman besar, musuh kecil — "Big friend, small enemy" | Making alliances matters more than nursing grudges. Grow your friends, shrink your enemies. |
| Datang baik, pulang baik — "Come good, go home good" | The rule of port hospitality. If you arrive in peace, you leave in peace. Violating this is the worst crime. |
| Sudah jual, sudah beli — "Already sold, already bought" | A deal is a deal. Once agreed, it is complete. No backing out. |
