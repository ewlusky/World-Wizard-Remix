## Cultural Context

Thurzokk is the language of the Iron Clans — nomadic warriors who measure status through combat, loyalty, and the honor of their bloodline. The language is deliberately efficient and harsh-sounding, with minimal politeness forms. There are no Thurzokk words for "please" or "I'm sorry." Instead, respect is expressed through action verbs: offering, shielding, giving. Thurzokk has an elaborate system of insults which function as formal challenges — to insult someone correctly is itself a martial art.

## Phonology & Grammar Rules

1. Consonants favor hard stops and gutturals: k, g, t, d, zh, kh, rr (uvular trill), and -kk (double-stop, pronounced with force).

2. Vowels are short and clipped: a (as in "cut"), o (as in "got"), u (as in "put"). No long vowels except in the honorific suffix -ōk.

3. Word order is Verb-Subject-Object: the action happens first. "Kills Thurzokk the enemy" is correct grammar.

4. Plurality is expressed by prefixing gruk- to the noun: grak (warrior) → grukgrak (warriors, a war-band).

5. There are three grammatical moods: Declarative (no marker), Challenging (suffix -kagh), and Boasting (suffix -ōkk). "I fight" vs "I fight you — try me!" vs "I fight — hear my glory."

6. Commands use the verb root alone with no subject: "Grakh!" = "Fight!" "Dorak!" = "Run!" "Sharokk!" = "Submit!"

7. Adjectives follow the noun and take the suffix -ak to agree: grak torak-ak (strong warrior).

8. Honorific address uses the suffix -ōk after a name or title. Using someone's name without -ōk to their face is a formal insult if they outrank you.

## Idioms & Cultural Sayings

| Idiom | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Kharr brak zorak — "Blood is not sky" | Lineage does not guarantee freedom or greatness. You must earn it. |
| Thoknak groth — "A coward chieftain" | The worst possible compound insult — a formal declaration that someone is unfit to lead. |
| Grakh brak dorak — "Fight without speed" | Brute force without strategy. A valid tactic, but an insult when said of someone who thinks they're clever. |
| Torak ōrgrak nakk druzhōk — "Even a champion shames the honored elder" | No victory justifies dishonoring one's betters. Used to criticize those who disrespect their own tradition. |
| Zorak thurzok — "Iron sky" | An impossible thing — the sky cannot be iron. Said of someone making absurd demands. |
