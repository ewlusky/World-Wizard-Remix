## Cultural Context

Tessavori is the language of the great merchant republics — city-states built on trade, contract, and the careful management of reputation. The language is flowing and musical on the surface, with elaborate honorific systems concealing precise legal and commercial meaning. Every greeting in Tessavori contains an implicit status evaluation. The language has a dedicated "contract register" used for formal agreements, which is grammatically separate from everyday speech.

## Phonology & Grammar Rules

1. Consonants are predominantly voiced and smooth: v, l, r (trilled lightly), m, n, s, z, and gl (the Italian "gli" sound, a palatal lateral). Hard consonants (k, t, p) appear mainly in loanwords.

2. Every word ends in a vowel. Final -o = masculine/neutral; final -a = feminine; final -i = plural; final -ë = formal/elevated register.

3. Vowels are pure: a, e, i, o, u. The combination -io- becomes a glide (like "yo"); -ia- becomes "ya."

4. Stress falls on the penultimate syllable by default, with an accent mark (á) indicating exceptions.

5. The honorific system has five levels: (1) nomi (commoner), (2) tessare (respected citizen), (3) domini (merchant-lord), (4) sérvitori-mástra (master contractor), and (5) ávori-sublime (the highest merchants, addressed in the third person).

6. Verb agreement tracks three persons and two numbers, with an additional "civic plural" (noi-tessare) for speaking on behalf of a guild or city.

7. Negation uses the double negative (ne...mai, ne...niente) and is not grammatically "wrong" — it is emphatic.

8. The "conditional of courtesy" (using the -êssori suffix) is used for requests and offers. Demanding without it is a serious social breach.

## Phrases & Expressions

| Phrase | Literal Translation | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Vostro grazia, domini | "Your grace, merchant-lord" | Formal greeting to a superior |
| Contraté solvori, avori-tessare | "The contract is settled, honored citizen" | Business is concluded satisfactorily |
| Imbroglio mai, per fraternori | "No entanglement, for brotherhood's sake" | Let's not complicate this — we are allies |
| Obligazori mia, sublimori | "My obligation, transcendent one" | I acknowledge my debt to you |
| Vendêtta tessare-nomi | "A citizen's rightful claim on a commoner" | Formal declaration of grievance (legal) |
| Profitto grazia-syl | "May their profit carry grace" | Well-wishing for a business partner's success |
| Magnificori, testori prêgo? | "Magnificent one, might I request?" | Most polite form of a business request |

## Idioms & Cultural Sayings

| Idiom | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Avori non si vende — "Ivory does not sell itself" | Value must be demonstrated; worth must be communicated. You cannot be passive about your own merit. |
| Imbroglio porta imbroglio — "Entanglement brings entanglement" | Getting involved in complicated schemes leads to more complications. Mind your contracts. |
| Grazia oggi, obligazori domani — "Grace today, obligation tomorrow" | Every favor creates a debt. Never accept something freely — know what you'll owe. |
| Fraternori nella tasca — "Brotherhood in the pocket" | Cynical phrase: people claim fellowship when they want money. Beware false comrades. |
| Vendêtta è il conto aperto — "A grievance is an open account" | Grudges are debts that compound interest. Settle them quickly or they ruin you. |
