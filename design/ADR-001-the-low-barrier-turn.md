# ADR-001: The low-barrier turn is a design pillar

**Status:** accepted
**Date:** 2026-08-09
**Context:** recorded during the v2.2.1 port, from the designer's account of why these rules exist

---

## Decision

**Lowering the barrier to taking a turn is a first-class design value, equal to the other four.** The wheel, the right to skip, and the Quiet Turn are one system serving that goal, and they are not to be simplified away in the name of a tighter loop.

## Why this needed writing down

`DESIGN-DIRECTION.md` listed five design values — the map is canon, mess is interesting, small things matter, reserves are poetic, language belongs to the world — and none of them was the reason the most distinctive rules in this game exist. The wheel was not mentioned in that document at all, despite being the mechanic a new player meets first.

That gap is how a rule gets "cleaned up" by someone who cannot see what it was load-bearing for.

## The origin

The family edition was played with a player who was getting visibly self-conscious about taking a turn: not about the rules, but about *deciding what to add* while everyone waited. The freeze was never lack of interest. It was an unbounded creative choice made in front of an audience.

The insight was that this player did not need fewer rules or gentler rules. They needed **the first decision made for them**, so they could get to the second decision, which is the enjoyable one. Narrowing the question from "invent something for this world" to "add a magical terrain feature" is the entire intervention.

## What it produced

Three rules, which look like unrelated conveniences and are not:

1. **🎡 Spin the Wheel / 🎯 Choose.** The wheel's segments are table names. It answers *what kind of thing* so the player only has to answer *which one*, and even that can be rolled. Presented as a permanent equal fork, never as the beginner ramp — the moment it reads as the easy mode, using it becomes an admission.
2. **You may skip your turn.** No cost, no penalty, nothing owed. A pass with a price attached is not a pass.
3. **The Quiet Turn.** A turn can be one added detail or one drawn connection, for no AP and no Tally. Explicitly a complete contribution, not a partial one.

## Consequences

- **Randomness is always optional and always overridable.** "Make something up" stays legal at every step. The wheel is scaffolding, not a generator; a spin the player ignores because it gave them a better idea has succeeded.
- **Never gate any of the three behind a player type, age, or experience level.** No "simplified variant for younger or casual players" that quarantines them. That reintroduces the exact self-consciousness these rules exist to remove. This settles the "family variant" open question in favour of one ruleset that scales.
- **Anti-hoarding and pacing rules must not punish small turns.** A per-turn AP pool is fine; a rule that makes a Quiet Turn cost you something is not.
- **The wheel needs a shipped form.** Printed wheel, card deck, or build-your-own instructions is still open. Whatever ships, the Spin/Choose fork appears in the core turn sequence, not in an appendix of variants.
- **Verify against a hesitant player, not an eager one.** Playtesting the loop with people who enjoy open creative prompts will not detect a regression here.

## Note on sourcing

The specific person and situation behind this are private and are recorded outside this repository. The public framing is "a player who is hesitant to invent on demand," which is the general case and the one the design should serve.
