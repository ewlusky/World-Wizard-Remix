# Games Research

*Mechanics surveyed during design. Each entry notes what's worth stealing and what doesn't fit.*

---

## Dawn of Worlds (Dave Bishop et al.)
**Direct ancestor.** Free PDF, AP-based god-game, 2–4 players, three Ages.

**Key mechanics:**
- 2d6 power *per turn* (not per Age)
- **Anti-hoarding rule:** end a turn with ≤5 power → cumulative +1 on next roll (up to +3). Discourages banking; rewards spending.
- Age-tiered costs: terrain cheap in Age 1, races/avatars cheap in Age 2, armies/wonders cheap in Age 3
- **Command Race** — spend 4 PP to order a People to migrate, attack, or build
- **Avatar** — physical manifestation of a god's will; Create costs 10 PP
- **Cataclysm** — 15+ PP destructive action
- No Pass tax; the anti-hoarding rule handles incentive alignment instead

**What we're keeping:** Age-tiered cost idea, anti-hoarding bonus  
**What we're changing:** We add a Pass tax (Price of Passing) for narrative texture; our AP economy is per-Age now but likely moving per-turn in the publishable edition

## Microscope (Ben Robbins, Lame Mage Productions)
**The canonical no-GM history game.** Fractal structure, non-chronological.

**Key mechanics:**
- **Big Picture** — one-line thematic statement agreed at the start
- **Palette** — before play, each player silently adds one ⭕ YES and one ❌ NO to a shared list. YES items may appear (and are encouraged). NO items cannot appear. Freezes when any player declines to add more. *Best way to include a child's taste preferences without requiring them to argue for or against anything.*
- **Lens** — rotating player who establishes the round's Focus
- **Legacy** — after exploring a Focus, the next player names one thread they want to echo; their following contribution must relate to it
- **Pushing** — a player can challenge another's contribution; actor either revises or stands firm; group votes; ties go to the actor. "Collaboration through the medium of the game, rather than through open discussion."
- **Fractal:** Period → Event → Scene, can zoom in or out non-chronologically

**What we're taking:** Palette (solves the young-player content-boundary problem), Legacy Threads, Pushing  
**What we're not:** Full fractal structure is too abstract for casual family play; Microscope is dense, ours needs to stay scannable

## The Quiet Year (Avery Alder, Buried Without Ceremony)
**52-card map game.** A community has one quiet year before a threat arrives.

**Key mechanics:**
- 52 cards = 52 weeks; suits = seasons (hearts Spring, diamonds Summer, clubs Fall, spades Winter)
- **Each card presents two questions; player picks one.** Constrained creative choice beats blank-page freedom.
- **Abundance/Scarcity setup:** one resource starts abundant; all others start scarce. Customized per game, no preset list.
- Three actions per turn: Start a Project / Discover Something New / Hold a Discussion
- **Project dice:** d6 placed on the hex, counts down each week. Anyone can accelerate or sabotage. Visible time on the physical map.
- **Frost Shepherds:** King of Spades, somewhere in the final 13 cards. Game ends immediately when drawn. Unknown-when ending. *Transforms every late-game turn from routine to loaded.*
- **Contempt Tokens:** silent registry of frustration. No in-game effect. (Reviewers are divided on whether these work; skip for WorldWizard.)

**What we're taking:** Frost Shepherds hidden countdown, Abundance/Scarcity Age declaration, Project dice concept  
**What we're adapting:** Two-question card structure as design inspiration for the Oracle's Handful

## Wanderhome (Jay Dragon, Possum Creek Games)
**Pastoral animal-folk travel RPG.** Built on Belonging Outside Belonging engine (no dice, no GM).

**Key mechanics (BoB engine):**
- **Weak Moves = gain a token.** Doing something that complicates your situation, steps outside comfort, or accepts difficulty earns a token.
- **Strong Moves = spend a token.** Good outcomes, overcoming threats, helping others costs tokens.
- *The economy rewards characters for accepting complication.* This is the design heart of Complicate in WorldWizard.
- **Playbooks with Traits/Nature** — not classes, but tendencies
- **Holiday advancement:** characters advance between seasons at named holiday points, not via XP accrual
- **Kith:** ordinary residents of places the travelers pass through. Named people living ordinary lives. "Worlds feel alive when ground-level named individuals exist alongside the epic."

**What we're taking:** Complicate as Weak Move analog, Kith layer concept, Holiday between Ages  
**What doesn't fit:** The full BoB engine is too structurally different from our AP economy to graft on; we're borrowing the design philosophy, not the mechanics

## The Ground Itself (Everest Pipkin)
**One-session game about a place over time.** Coin + d6 + deck of cards.

**Key mechanic:**
- **Declared "Now" scale.** Group announces at the start what one "moment" means. Playing on millennia means 100 years is "now." Playing on decades means a season is "now." This reframes every action to feel appropriately scaled.
- Four time cycles

**What we're taking:** The declared Now scale per Age — this is the core of our logarithmic Age structure  
**What doesn't fit:** The full game is a single short arc; ours spans sessions and years of real-world play

## Dialect (Kathryn Hymes & Hakan Seyalioglu, Thorny Games)
**A game about language and how it dies.** 3–5 players, single session.

**Key mechanics:**
- Three Ages, each a phase of the isolated community's life
- **Language Cards:** drawn each turn, prompt a new word for an object, event, concept, or feeling the community needs to name. Collaboratively coin the word.
- **Make a Connection:** relate the Language Card to one of the community's defining Aspects
- The community's language (and community) dies at the end — the finite arc is structural
- Created by actual linguists; the language evolution is linguistically plausible

**What we're taking:** Coin-a-word mechanic for the LANGUAGE action family; the idea that a People's language evolves (and eventually can die)  
**What we're adapting:** Not a session game; the language layer in WorldWizard spans multiple Ages and Peoples

## Ex Novo (Sharkbomb Studios, Martin Nerurkar)
**City-building game.** Players are the *genius loci* — the spirit of a place.

**Key mechanic:**
- Players don't play gods above the city; they *are* the city's spirit
- Settlement grows through eras from founding to metropolis
- Prompts and dice guide narrative interpretation

**What we're taking:** Genus Loci turn mechanic — once per Age, spend 1 AP to SPEAK AS a place. After that, the place is a named Genus Loci and earns ⭐ for future interactions.  
**What doesn't fit:** Full framing (you are the spirit, not a god) conflicts with our Steward structure; ours is a single-turn visit, not a permanent identity shift

---

## Beak, Feather & Bone (Austin Walker)
**Short map-drawing game.** Corvid factions claiming buildings in an abandoned town.

**Key mechanic:**
- **Split authorship:** one player draws, another names, a third claims. Three-way collaborative ownership on every single feature.

**What we're taking:** The insight that Weave Bonus is better if mandated at creation time. Possible future mechanic: any new creation must be *named* by the player to your left before it's inked.  
**What we're not:** The game is extremely short (30–60 min); our time structure is long-form

## Key design patterns observed across all sources

- **Unresolved mysteries beat resolved lore.** Games stay interesting by deepening what's unknown, not explaining it.
- **Unknown-when endings create tension that fixed counters can't.** (Frost Shepherds, vs. "Tally hits 14.")
- **Constraint breeds creativity.** "Pick one of two options" (Quiet Year cards) produces better play than blank choice.
- **Named individuals > populations.** One named Kith does more narrative work than a census.
- **Geography creates plot gravity.** Rivers, coasts, mountains shape story without scripting it.
- **Contradictions are features.** Let two sources disagree; the disagreement is the texture.
- **Return to places.** Worlds feel alive when the same hex shows up across three Ages with three different meanings.
- **Advancement via ritual beats advancement via accumulation.** (Wanderhome Holidays > XP grind.)
