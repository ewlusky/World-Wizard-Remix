# The AP Economy

Action Points are the only currency. You roll them once per Age and spend them down. The interesting part is the four ways the economy bends: two that give AP back, one that pays you for cooperating, and one that costs nothing at all.

## The Price of Passing

You can always Pass for more AP. The world does not give you time for free.

1. Roll 1d6. Gain that many AP, plus 1 for every 2 Avatars, magic sources, or Peoples you control.
2. Roll on [Table 9: Disasters](../tables/09-disasters.md) and on the Disaster Scope table.
3. **The disaster lands on something of yours.** Pick 4 of your own creations the rolled disaster could plausibly strike, then roll 1d4 to see which one takes it. Follow whatever the disaster implies and change the map accordingly.
4. **Describe it in detail.** You did not get those AP for nothing.
5. **Bold the hex.** Darken the lines around one hex at the disaster's location so the scar is visible on the map forever.

Why this way: Passing should feel like a bargain, not a loophole. The AP is real, the cost is real, and the cost is *your own stuff*. The map gets weirder, scarier and more lived-in as a direct result of people needing more turns.

**If you have nothing to lose yet** — early in an Age, no creations of your own — you may Pass once for free. Describe a world event instead.

## Complicate

The inverse of Pass. Where Pass damages a creation for AP, Complicate *deepens* one.

Pick a creation you Steward and give it one of these:

- a **flaw** — they are brave, but will never speak first
- a **contradiction** — the river runs north by day and south by night
- a **mystery** — nobody remembers who founded this city
- an **internal rift** — half the tribe will not eat the other half's bread
- a **limitation** — the magic only works under the moon
- a **private sorrow** — their first god has gone silent, and none of them will admit it

Write it into the creation's note. Gain **+1 AP** immediately. Any one creation may be complicated **once per Age**.

Why this way: the best creations *want* trouble. Complicating a People makes them people. Complicating a magical feature makes it magical. A world of unflawed wonders is a world that has stopped wondering about itself.

## The Weave Bonus

The mechanic that makes the map come alive.

When your action **uses** or **connects to** something another Steward made, and that Steward agrees to it, put a small star on the hex. You have made a **Convergence**. Two things happen:

- the +1† surcharge for touching their work **does not apply**, and
- the Steward who agreed gets **+1 AP**.

No tracking, no ledger, no separate currency. A star on the map and a note to yourself.

Stars stack. One turn can earn several, one per distinct other-Steward creation it touches, and there is no cap. That is deliberate: the cheapest turn in the game is the one that ties existing things together, which is exactly the turn that makes a world out of a pile of stuff.

## The Quiet Turn

Any time. No AP. No Tally. Walk past the map without the energy for a full action and you can still:

- **add a detail** to something already there — a name, a tradition, a rumor, a story
- **connect two things** with one sentence in someone's history
- **sketch something small** on an existing hex — a footprint, a watchfire, a carving

Quiet Turns are how the world breathes between the big moves. They are also, along with [the wheel and the right to skip](01-how-to-play.md#why-the-wheel-exists), the reason someone who does not want to invent a civilization today can still take part today.

A Quiet Turn is not a lesser turn. Some of the best things on a map arrive this way.
